Nanded – On the Banks of the Godavari

Nanded is a major city in Maharashtra's Marathwada region, known for the sacred Takht Sachkhand Sri Hazur Sahib Gurudwara, ancient forts and temples, and its setting on the Godavari River.

Faith • History • Godavari • Forts

City overview & history

Nanded is one of Maharashtra's larger cities and the main centre of its district, located in the southeastern part of the state along the Godavari River in the Marathwada region.

Historically the area has connections to ancient dynasties such as the Nandas and Mauryas, later Deccan sultanates and Maratha rule, which led to the construction of forts, temples and river ghats around the city.

The city holds exceptional importance in Sikh history because Guru Gobind Singh Ji spent his final days here and declared the Guru Granth Sahib as the eternal Guru at Hazur Sahib.

Quick facts

  • Region: Marathwada, southeastern Maharashtra.
  • River: Located on the banks of the Godavari.
  • Key identity: Major Sikh pilgrimage centre and district headquarters.
  • Languages: Marathi, Urdu, Hindi and others used locally.

Climate & best time to visit

Nanded has a tropical climate with very hot summers, heavy monsoon rains and relatively cool winters.

The most comfortable time to visit for sightseeing and pilgrim trips is usually from October or November to February, when days are pleasant and evenings can feel cool.

Summers can see temperatures rising well above 40 degrees Celsius, while monsoon months bring strong showers that can affect travel plans in the district.

Best season: Nov – Feb.
Summer highs: Around 40–42°C.
Monsoon: Heavy rain July – Sept.

Major attractions & experiences

Visitors come to Nanded mainly to pay respects at Hazur Sahib, explore historic forts and temples, and spend time at river ghats and nearby waterfalls and hill shrines.

Important sites include Sikh gurudwaras, Mahur's Renuka Devi temple, Nanded Fort, Kandhar Fort, Sahastrakund waterfall and other heritage and nature spots across the district.

Culture, food & lifestyle

Nanded blends influences from Marathi, Sikh and Deccan cultures, visible in its festivals, places of worship and everyday street life around bazaars and riverfront areas.

The city's food scene includes traditional Maharashtrian dishes along with flavours shaped by nearby Telangana, plus busy evening street-food zones and local markets.

Handicrafts and textiles, along with agricultural products from the district, are popular purchases for visitors exploring local markets.

Why Nanded stands out

  • One of the holiest cities for Sikhs due to Hazur Sahib and related history.
  • Gateway to Mahur hills, historic forts and waterfalls across the district.
  • Strong mix of pilgrimage, heritage and typical Marathwada city life on the Godavari.

Travel tips for visitors

Many visitors plan 2–3 days in and around Nanded, combining darshan at Hazur Sahib with trips to Mahur, local forts and nearby nature spots, using autos, shared transport or hired cabs.

  • Prefer winter months for long outdoor darshan and sightseeing; carry light woollens for evenings.
  • In summer, schedule temple and fort visits in the early morning or later evening to avoid peak heat.
  • During monsoon, check river and waterfall conditions, as heavy rain can affect access to certain spots.
